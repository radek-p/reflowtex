\begin{theorem}
Every natural number above one has a prime factor.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose not, and take the least counterexample $n$.
\begin{claim}
The number $n$ is not prime, since a prime is its own prime factor.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
A prime $p$ divides itself, so it would have a prime factor after all.
\end{proof}
So $n = ab$ with $1 < a < n$, and $a$, being smaller, has a prime factor,
which divides $n$ too.
\end{proof}
