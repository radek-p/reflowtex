\section*{Outer gaps close, internal gaps hold}
A one-column display carries its centring in the alignment's outer tabskips.
Those have ink on one side only, so they close completely and the display is
never held wider than the formula itself:
\begin{align*}
\text{functional rational relations} \quad \subseteq \quad \text{unambiguous rational relations.}
\end{align*}
Narrow the column past that width and the display freezes flush with the left
margin, peeks into whatever margin the page allows, and scrolls for the rest.

A numbered equation adds a gap with ink on both sides – the formula on one, its
number on the other. That one stops at the configured minimum instead of
closing, so the number never runs into the equation:
\begin{equation}
  \text{unambiguous rational relations} \quad \subseteq \quad \text{bimachines.}
\end{equation}
